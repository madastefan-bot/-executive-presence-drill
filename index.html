01 · Sounds

Make the key sounds cleaner.

0:00–1:00

Clarity beats accent imitation. Practise the features below with a light, controlled touch.

Drop the post-vocalic R quarter → quaw-tuh

In non-rhotic British English, an r after a vowel is usually not pronounced unless the next sound is a vowel.

quarter → quaw-tuh
car → cah
order → aw-duh
part → paht
“We’ll review the quarter figures before the board call.”
Use the long open “ah” can't → kahnt

In many standard Southern British pronunciations, the vowel in can't, after, staff, half and chance is long and open. Sustain the vowel rather than using a short Romanian “a”.

can't → kahnt
after → ahf-tuh
staff → stahf
half → hahf
“I can’t commit to that timing until after the tax review.”
Put the tongue between the teeth for TH the → thuh

For th, the tongue tip lightly touches or comes just between the teeth. For the, this, that and they, use the voiced /ð/ sound rather than t, d, s or z.

the → thuh
this → dhis
that → dhat
they → dhay
“That’s the thing I’d like us to think through before we decide.”
02 · Rhythm

Stop giving every word equal weight.

1:00–2:00

English is stress-timed: important words are longer, clearer and stronger while function words compress. Think DA-da-da, not DA-DA-DA-DA.

I NEED the NUMbers by THURSday.
Tap a stressed word for a pulse.
We CAN take that OFFline for NOW.
Compress “that / for”.
I’d PUSH back GENtly on THAT.
Make “gently” lighter, not louder.
